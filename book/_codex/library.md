# Library and citations

Use this note for citation metadata, source access, quotations, literature
PDFs, and durable source notes. For selective PDF intake and reading, also
follow `book/_codex/literature_workflow.md`.

## Roles

- `book/citations/author.bib` contains author-maintained entries.
- `book/citations/agent.bib` contains vetted entries added by agents.
- `book/literature/` holds local PDFs and companion source notes.
- Wiki pages synthesize across sources; they do not replace canonical source
  notes or bibliography records.
- Put temporary extractions and page images under `scratch/`.

## Bibliography policy

- Before adding a source, search both bibliography files for duplicates by
  DOI, title, and author-year.
- Verify authors, title, venue or publisher, year, volume, issue, pages, DOI,
  and edition against Crossref, the publisher, a library catalog, or another
  primary bibliographic record.
- Never invent a DOI, page range, issue, edition, or citation key.
- Preserve the author's choice between narrative and parenthetical citation
  unless editing the sentence substantively.
- Narrative citation: `@key`.
- Parenthetical citation: `[@key]`.
- Attach page locators to quotations and page-specific claims whenever
  possible.

## PDFs and source notes

- Inventory `book/literature/` before searching elsewhere or reading every
  file deeply.
- Name a PDF and its companion note with the same citekey stem, such as
  `literature/example2024.pdf` and `literature/example2024.md`.
- For a book split into chapter files, use one citekey-named folder with a
  short book-level note and clearly named chapter notes.
- A source note should record:
  - citation key and verified title;
  - source and access status;
  - why the source matters;
  - main claims and the evidence supporting them;
  - useful quotations with locators;
  - limitations, disagreements, and items still unverified;
  - where the source is used or might be used.
- Keep source notes concise enough to scan. Link to the PDF rather than
  turning the note into a full-text duplicate.

## Evidence and quotation checks

- Distinguish metadata verification from full-text verification.
- Do not treat a search snippet, abstract, review, or other source's citation
  as proof of what the underlying source says.
- For an important claim, record what evidence carries it: data, cases,
  comparisons, archival records, tables, figures, interviews, or reasoning.
- Verify quotations against the PDF page or page image. Preserve exact
  wording, capitalization, ellipses, and meaningful context.
- Distinguish printed page numbers from PDF-page ordinals. Record both when
  they differ and the distinction matters.
- If a quotation or claim reaches the project through a secondary source,
  note the trace-to-primary task before final drafting.
- Record partial coverage honestly: metadata-only, abstract-only,
  snippet-only, selected chapters checked, partial PDF, full text checked, or
  wrong edition.

## Source access

- Prefer lawful public copies, publisher pages, repositories, library
  catalogs, institutional access, and author copies.
- For books and older works, check library catalogs and controlled digital
  lending when available.
- Access to an online reader is not the same as a downloaded local file. Say
  that a source is local only when the file exists in `book/literature/`.
- If the needed edition or language differs from the accessible copy, record
  the mismatch rather than forcing page references to fit.
- When a paper relies on unavailable data or materials, suggest contacting the
  corresponding author rather than silently substituting a weaker source.
- Keep a short, ranked retrieval backlog for consequential inaccessible
  sources. Record the exact question each source could answer, current access
  status, failed route and date, and best next step. Deduplicate it against
  existing notes.

## Citation discipline in prose

- Put citations where they support the claim, mechanism, comparison, or
  limitation—not only at the end of a long paragraph containing several
  different claims.
- Do not cite a source for a broader claim than it establishes.
- Separate the source's finding from the manuscript's interpretation.
- Use a visible placeholder such as `page to verify` when the source is known
  but the exact locator remains unresolved; do not drop the citation.
- Before handoff, check that every new citekey exists in one bibliography,
  metadata are verified, and quoted or page-specific claims have locators.
